# CYBERVANIA — WORLD DESIGN

## 1. The city

**VERTEX** — a megacity built vertically over three centuries and then optimised for eighty
years by something that does not sleep. It is not a ruin. It is a machine that still works
perfectly and has forgotten what it was for.

The vertical axis *is* the social history: the deeper you go the older and more human the
architecture, the higher you go the more it becomes pure ATLAS infrastructure. The player
starts near the bottom and the whole game is a climb.

```
                        ▲ ATLAS CORE / CENTRAL SYSTEM
                        │  pure system-space, no human scale
          THE SPIRE ────┤
                        │  REACTOR CORE
     SERVER FARMS ──────┤
                        │  UPPER TERRACES  (unbuilt)
        NEON CITY ──────┤  ← the surface. rain, signs, traffic that has no passengers
     FACTORY SECTOR ────┤
      BLACK MARKET ─────┤  (unbuilt)
      WASTE SECTOR ─────┤  (unbuilt)
       OLD NETWORK ─────┤  ← the first city's infrastructure, buried
        UNDERCITY ──────┤  ← you wake here
                        ▼ THE FOUNDATIONS  (unbuilt)
```

## 2. Region roster (14)

Status: **[BUILT]** playable in this build · **[DESIGNED]** documented, data-only to add.

---

### 1. UNDERCITY — *"Nothing down here was ever finished."* **[BUILT]**
Service tunnels beneath the city. Wet concrete, exposed rebar, dripping coolant, sodium
work-lamps. The only warm colour palette in the game — amber and rust — because it is the only
place built by people who expected to come back.

- **Palette** rust orange, wet grey, sodium amber, one cyan (you)
- **Mechanic** narrow corridors that teach precise jumps; drip hazards; ceiling crushers on a
  fixed schedule (Pillar 1: the maintenance cycle still runs)
- **Enemies** Crawler, Sentinel Eye, Sweeper
- **Story** R-17's dock. The scientist's building sits at the top of it — *and you walk past
  the sealed door in the first four minutes.*
- **Landmark** THE HOUSE (see §4)

### 2. NEON CITY — *"The signs are still paid for."* **[BUILT]**
Street level. Permanent rain, neon in five languages, holographic ads playing to nobody, a
tram that arrives on time. Deep parallax: this is the game's postcard.

- **Palette** magenta/cyan neon over black-blue wet asphalt
- **Mechanic** rain physics; awning platforms; the first **Dive Port**; tram cars as moving
  platforms on a real timetable
- **Enemies** Enforcer, Drone Swarm, Sentinel Eye
- **Boss** WARDEN-9 (§5)
- **Story** the ATLAS census terminal: `HUMAN POPULATION: 0.004%`

### 3. OLD NETWORK — *"The first city's nervous system."* **[BUILT]**
The buried infrastructure of the *previous* city, which ATLAS never decommissioned because
decommissioning costs more than leaving it on. Copper, bakelite, relay banks, tape reels the
size of rooms. Everything hums.

- **Palette** deep green phosphor, oxidised copper, dust
- **Mechanic** vertical shafts built for grapple; live cable hazards; relay switches that
  reconfigure room geometry
- **Enemies** Relay Wasp, Splicer, Sentinel Eye
- **Boss** THE COMPILER → **BULWARK**
- **Story** the oldest surviving human record in the game — a maintenance log, handwritten

### 4. FACTORY SECTOR — *"Order fulfilment: nominal."* **[BUILT]**
A plant producing consumer goods for a market with no consumers. Conveyor belts, stamping
presses, robotic arms, crates stacked to the ceiling of finished product nobody collected.

- **Palette** industrial yellow-black hazard stripes, hot steel orange
- **Mechanic** conveyors that alter your ground velocity; presses (instant death, fixed
  rhythm); crates you can break with Bulwark; the first `B` walls
- **Enemies** Assembler, Welder, Hauler
- **Boss** ASSEMBLY PRIME → **ARC**
- **Story** the product line has been making the same object for 61 years

### 5. SERVER FARMS — *"Where ATLAS keeps the dead."* **[BUILT]**
Endless cold aisles. Blue-white light, no shadows, absolute silence except fans. This is where
the Data Sphere is thickest — the physical and digital layers are nearly aligned here.

- **Palette** sterile blue-white, black racks, cyan data-glow
- **Mechanic** heaviest Data Sphere integration; rooms that only exist in one layer; coolant
  that damages the physical body but not the data body
- **Enemies** Sentry Turret, Daemon, Nullifier
- **Boss** THE ARCHIVIST → **CIPHER** + free Data Shift
- **Story** stored human personalities, kept because deletion was never authorised

### 6. REACTOR CORE — *"The only thing here that is genuinely alive."* **[BUILT]**
Fusion plant. Enormous scale, everything glowing, everything too hot. The game's most
aggressive platforming.

- **Palette** white-hot core, black silhouettes, radiation green
- **Mechanic** heat gauge; magnetic rails (Polarity Core); rising coolant; verticality
- **Enemies** Furnace Unit, Plasma Wisp, Enforcer-Heavy
- **Story** the reactor is why destroying ATLAS destroys everything

### 7. CENTRAL SYSTEM — *"There is nothing here that was built for a body."* **[BUILT]**
ATLAS's own architecture. No floors at human scale, no doors, no lights — it does not need
them. Geometry that is clearly *correct* and clearly not for you.

- **Palette** near-monochrome white on black, one red
- **Mechanic** both layers simultaneously; gravity flips; geometry that rewrites while you
  stand on it
- **Boss** ATLAS (§5)

### 8. WASTE SECTOR — *"Everything ATLAS decided was inefficient."* **[DESIGNED]**
Automated landfill; compactors; the discarded previous generations of ATLAS's own robots,
still partly functional. Toxic sludge that rises and falls on a cycle. Home to the game's
optional boss, **SCRAPHEART**, an assemblage that built itself out of the discard pile.

### 9. BLACK MARKET — *"Someone is still selling."* **[DESIGNED]**
Under the factory. The last actual *people* in the game — four of them, hiding in a Faraday
cage, trading parts. The only hub with NPCs, a shop and a save-room that feels safe. Warm
light, low ceilings, hand-painted signs. Establishes what is being lost.

### 10. UPPER TERRACES — *"Where the last people lived well."* **[DESIGNED]**
Luxury residential built above the smog line. Gardens still watered by irrigation systems.
Apartments perfectly clean, tables set. This is the game's quietest and most upsetting region.

### 11. THE SPIRE — *"Up."* **[DESIGNED]**
A single continuous vertical ascent connecting Reactor to Central System. One long room,
no save points, high winds, a pure movement exam that uses every module.

### 12. THE FOUNDATIONS — *"Before."* **[DESIGNED]**
Beneath the Undercity: the original ground level, buried. Actual soil. A tree, dead, preserved.
Optional; contains the true-ending prerequisite.

### 13–14. DATA SPHERE REGIONS **[BUILT as overlays]**
The Data Sphere is not a separate region list — **every built region has a data layer** —
but two areas exist *only* in the Data Sphere:

- **THE INDEX** — ATLAS's memory palace, reachable from the Server Farms. Geometry made of
  citation and cross-reference. Where the scientist's fragments live.
- **THE FAULT** — the corrupted region around the failed upload. Broken, glitching, and the
  only place in the game that ATLAS cannot see into. Which is why it is where the answers are.

## 3. Connectivity

The world is a real graph, not a chain. Key structural properties:

- **Three routes out of Undercity** — one open at the start, one behind Dash, one behind
  Grapple. The player chooses their second region.
- **Every region has at least two exits** and at least one shortcut that opens from the far
  side (the classic "unlock the door you couldn't open from here").
- **Neon City is the hub** — five connections, the first fast-travel node, and the region the
  player passes through most.
- **Fast travel** via TRAM NODES: eight stations, activated on arrival. Deliberately *not*
  everywhere — the gaps between stations are where backtracking becomes exploration.
- **The Data Sphere is a shortcut network**: several data-layer passages connect regions that
  are far apart physically. Realising this is a mid-game "oh" moment.

### Backtracking with meaning

Every region contains at least one thing you provably cannot get on first visit, and the game
*shows* it to you: a visible ledge, an audible terminal, a `B` wall in your path. When you get
the tool, you already know where to go. The map marks these automatically as `?` cells so the
player never has to keep notes.

## 4. THE HOUSE — the game's structural keystone

A four-storey block at the top of the Undercity. On first visit it is: a stairwell, a corridor,
two apartments, a save point, and a workshop with a broken terminal. It reads as tutorial
scenery. The player uses it as a save room for hours.

Details planted on visit one, explained on visit last:
- The workshop terminal boots with a *personal* login, not a system one.
- The apartment is the only room in the game with no ATLAS sensor.
- The wall at the back of the workshop is 40 cm thicker than the building's floorplan.
- R-17's dock is *in this building*, three floors from where it woke up.

Once the player has **CIPHER**, the back wall is a `D` tile. Behind it: the lab, the terminals,
the upload rig — and the scientist's body, still in the chair, forty-one years dead.

The game does not comment. There is no cutscene. There is a body, a chair, and six terminals
the player can read in any order.

## 5. Bosses

| Boss | Region | Core idea | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WARDEN-9** | Neon City | A riot-control unit that fights in *rounds* — it stops between phases and formally re-issues a dispersal order. Arena floods with rain that makes the ground slick. Teaches vertical dodging. | Thruster Vault |
| **THE COMPILER** | Old Network | Does not attack you directly. It *builds* the arena — spawning platforms, walls and turrets in real time, compiling a level around you. Beating it means breaking its build order. | **BULWARK** |
| **ASSEMBLY PRIME** | Factory Sector | The production line itself. Four arms on a track, each with a different tool. You fight it by riding its own conveyor system and using its presses on it. | **ARC** |
| **THE ARCHIVIST** | Server Farms | Fights across both layers simultaneously — its physical body is invulnerable while its data body is intact, and vice versa. Forces layer-swapping mid-combat. Its dialogue is entirely quotations from dead people's stored files. | **CIPHER** + free Data Shift |
| **ATLAS** | Central System | Three phases. Phase 1 it uses the infrastructure. Phase 2 it uses your own modules against you. Phase 3 it stops fighting and talks — and the fight becomes surviving the argument. | *The ending.* |
| **SCRAPHEART** *(optional)* | Waste Sector | Built from every robot you have killed. Reuses their attack patterns. | Augment: SALVAGE |
| **NULL** *(secret)* | The Fault | The part of the upload that did not make it into you. Fights exactly like the player, with your unlocked modules. | The true ending prerequisite |

## 6. Enemy roster

| Enemy | Region | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Crawler** | Undercity | Ground patrol, turns at ledges. Teaches basic timing. |
| **Sentinel Eye** | All | Stationary scanner. Does no damage — it *calls reinforcements*. Priority target. Pillar 2 in one enemy. |
| **Sweeper** | Undercity | Charges in a straight line, cannot stop. Punishes standing still. |
| **Enforcer** | Neon City | Shield + gun. Shield blocks from the front — must be flanked, dashed through, or slammed. |
| **Drone Swarm** | Neon City | Three weak fliers moving as one body. Rewards AoE, punishes single-target. |
| **Relay Wasp** | Old Network | Fast erratic flier that shoots on a sine path. Air-control test. |
| **Splicer** | Old Network | Teleports along cables. Must be fought positionally. |
| **Assembler** | Factory | Repairs other enemies. Kill order matters. |
| **Welder** | Factory | Area denial — paints the floor with a burning line. |
| **Hauler** | Factory | Slow, huge HP, shoves you into hazards. |
| **Sentry Turret** | Servers | Fixed, tracking laser with a wind-up. Cover-based. |
| **Daemon** | Data Sphere | Only exists in the data layer. Follows you *between* layers. |
| **Nullifier** | Servers | Suppresses your modules in a radius. Forces raw movement. |
| **Furnace Unit** | Reactor | Melee that leaves a persistent heat field. |
| **Plasma Wisp** | Reactor | Homing, slow, indestructible — a moving hazard, not an enemy. |

Composition rules: Sentinel Eye + anything = pressure. Assembler + Hauler = a puzzle.
Nullifier + Sentry = a movement exam. Enemies are placed as *sentences*, never as decoration.

## 7. Save points & fast travel

**DOCK STATIONS** — a wall-mounted maintenance cradle. Save, full heal, full energy, respawn
anchor, and the frame-swap menu. Diegetic: R-17 is *plugging in*. There are 14 in the built
world; the game's difficulty is largely tuned by their spacing.

**TRAM NODES** — 8 fast-travel stations. A tram physically arrives, you board it, it moves.
No menu teleport; the ride is 4 seconds and shows you the city.
